# RELIANCE CONSENT ARCHITECTURE V1

**Document type:** Platform philosophy and business-rule architecture  
**Status:** Design baseline for future Reliance consent decisions  
**Relationship to the official audit:** The Current Consent, Privacy, and Recording Audit remains the official statement of the present platform baseline. This document defines the consent architecture that future design work should follow.

---

## Purpose

Reliance is a proof-of-service platform. Its purpose is to document work accurately while respecting the privacy, safety, dignity, and choices of everyone affected by the recording.

Reliance is not a marketplace. Consent must never be used as a device for advertising pressure, lead generation, forced public exposure, or ownership of a customer's identity.

This architecture is universal. It applies to services involving people, homes, businesses, vehicles, equipment, land, animals, documents, instruction, creative work, and future categories that do not yet exist.

The architecture is based on five ideas:

1. Permission to perform work is not permission to record.
2. Permission to record is not permission to publish.
3. Permission from one participant cannot replace permission from another participant who has separate authority.
4. A later withdrawal changes what may happen next, but it must not rewrite the historical truth of what was previously decided.
5. When authority, identity, scope, or audience is uncertain, Reliance chooses the narrower and more private path.

---

## 1. Universal Consent Model

Every Reliance recording decision must answer five questions:

1. **Purpose:** Why is the recording needed?
2. **Subject:** What people, property, activity, information, or location will be captured?
3. **Authority:** Who has the right to authorize that capture?
4. **Audience:** Who may see the result?
5. **Lifecycle:** What may be retained, withdrawn, restricted, disputed, or removed later?

Industry type does not decide the consent path. The answers to these five questions do.

A barber recording a haircut, a mechanic recording an engine repair, a tutor recording a lesson, and an electrician recording a panel replacement use the same architecture. Their subjects and privacy risks differ, but the governing decisions do not.

### The four separate permission layers

Reliance must treat the following as independent decisions:

1. **Service authority:** The service may be performed.
2. **Recording authority:** The defined service activity may be captured.
3. **Publication authority:** The approved result may be shown to a defined audience.
4. **Evidence retention authority:** Reliance may preserve the minimum historical record needed to establish what occurred and what decisions were made.

Approval at one layer does not automatically grant approval at another.

## 2. Participants in the Consent System

### Vendor

The vendor is the business responsible for the service, the work record, its staff, and the legitimate need for proof.

The vendor is accountable for:

- creating an accurate work record;
- identifying the real service purpose;
- assigning an authorized employee;
- ensuring the recording request is appropriate for the service;
- ensuring employees understand recording boundaries;
- honoring customer decisions;
- reviewing the proof for accuracy before requesting broader use; and
- responding to disputes, correction requests, and privacy concerns.

The vendor does not own the customer's consent, identity, likeness, premises, possessions, or private information.

### Employee

The employee is the person authorized by the vendor to perform or document the work.

The employee is accountable for:

- recording only the assigned work;
- staying within the approved purpose and scope;
- avoiding unnecessary people, information, and private areas;
- stopping when conditions no longer match the authorization;
- confirming that each recording accurately represents its service stage;
- reporting accidental capture or uncertainty; and
- refusing an instruction that would exceed consent or compromise safety.

Assignment gives the employee permission to act for the vendor. It does not give the employee independent authority over the customer or the customer's property.

### Customer

The customer is the person receiving the service or an authorized representative who has authority over the service location, subject, or affected private interests.

The customer has authority to:

- approve or decline recording when customer authority is required;
- define whether recording may include customer-controlled people, property, or information;
- choose whether approved proof remains private or may be published;
- review the actual media before public publication;
- withdraw future recording permission;
- withdraw or narrow publication permission;
- report misrepresentation, over-capture, or privacy harm; and
- dispute whether the recording stayed within the agreed scope.

A customer may act for another person only when the customer has legitimate authority to do so. A person cannot authorize the recording or publication of people, property, or rights they do not control.

### Admin

The admin is Reliance's neutral operational reviewer.

The admin has authority to:

- decide whether content is eligible to remain on Reliance;
- approve, reject, restrict, flag, or remove content from public display;
- require correction when proof does not meet platform standards;
- pause access during a privacy, safety, identity, or integrity dispute;
- review evidence of participant decisions; and
- enforce the platform's role boundaries consistently.

Admin approval can narrow or block use. It cannot create missing customer, vendor, employee, guardian, or third-party permission.

### Reliance

Reliance is the custodian of the consent system and the neutral keeper of proof.

Reliance is accountable for:

- defining consistent consent principles;
- separating service, recording, publication, and retention decisions;
- preserving reliable decision history;
- enforcing the most restrictive active permission;
- protecting participants from unauthorized audience expansion;
- providing neutral dispute and appeal paths;
- limiting evidence to legitimate proof-of-service purposes;
- preventing participant roles from being impersonated or merged; and
- maintaining the distinction between private proof and public promotion.

Reliance does not become the owner of the work, the customer, the vendor, or the recorded subject merely because it preserves proof.

### Protected non-participants

Bystanders, household members, employees of a customer, neighbors, children, visitors, and other incidentally affected people may not actively participate in the work record, but their privacy and dignity remain protected.

No active participant may assume authority over a protected non-participant. If a non-participant may be identifiable and valid authority is absent, the recording must avoid, pause, remove, obscure, or remain unavailable for broader viewing.

## 3. Authority and Decision Rights

| Participant | Authority held | Decisions permitted | Decisions never permitted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vendor | Business, service, staff, and work-record authority | Define legitimate proof purpose, assign employees, approve business representation, keep proof private, request publication, challenge inaccurate proof | Consent for a customer, guardian, bystander, or employee likeness; expand the audience beyond permission; rewrite a prior decision |
| Employee | Assigned service and moment-of-capture authority | Record within scope, pause or stop, confirm stage accuracy, report accidental capture, refuse unsafe or unauthorized recording | Change customer choices, publish independently, record a different job, continue after authorization is lost |
| Customer | Authority over the customer's decision, controlled location, private interests, and customer-facing publication | Approve or decline recording, set audience, approve exact media, withdraw future recording or publication, dispute scope | Authorize another person's rights without valid authority; alter genuine vendor evidence; compel public publication |
| Admin | Platform eligibility, safety, integrity, and moderation authority | Approve, reject, restrict, flag, unpublish, pause during disputes, enforce standards | Invent missing consent, impersonate a participant, widen audience, erase truthful decision history |
| Reliance | Consent-rule custody, evidence stewardship, platform neutrality, and safety authority | Define universal rules, preserve decision history, enforce restrictions, suspend distribution, resolve platform-status questions | Treat silence as consent, bundle unrelated permissions, favor a party by changing evidence, use proof beyond its stated purpose |

### Authority is specific, not general

Authority must be valid for:

- the particular work record;
- the particular service;
- the particular location;
- the particular recording purpose;
- the particular subject matter;
- the particular employee or authorized team;
- the particular time period; and
- the particular audience.

General permission from a previous service, a business relationship, an account, or an earlier recording does not automatically carry forward.

---

## 4. Decisions That May Never Be Delegated

The following decisions must remain with the participant who holds the underlying authority:

1. **Customer recording decision:** A vendor, employee, admin, or Reliance may not approve customer-controlled recording for the customer.
2. **Customer publication decision:** No other participant may make customer-controlled content public on the customer's behalf.
3. **Exact-media approval:** Approval of a general concept may not replace the customer's decision about the actual media when publication is requested.
4. **Guardian or representative authority:** A person claiming to act for someone else must have valid authority. That status may not be assumed or self-created by another participant.
5. **Employee personal-safety decision:** No participant may compel an employee to continue recording when the employee reasonably believes recording is unsafe, unlawful, or outside scope.
6. **Employee likeness decision:** If an employee is personally identifiable beyond ordinary work activity, another participant may not grant public use of that employee's identity.
7. **Vendor business representation:** A customer or employee may not independently publish proof as an official representation of the vendor through Reliance.
8. **Admin eligibility decision:** A vendor or customer may not bypass neutral review when Reliance review is required.
9. **Reliance rule stewardship:** No individual participant may waive safeguards that protect other participants or protected non-participants.

Execution may be delegated. Accountability and personal authority may not.

Each responsibility has one primary owner. Other participants may assist, but support does not transfer accountability.

## 6. Permissions Required Before Recording Begins

Recording may begin only when every applicable gate is satisfied.

### Gate 1: A real service purpose exists

The recording must relate to a legitimate work record and a defined proof-of-service purpose. Recording may not be created merely to gather promotional footage, monitor a person, or collect unrelated information.

### Gate 2: The vendor is accountable

The vendor must stand behind the work record, the assigned employee, and the stated purpose. A recording request must not exist without an accountable vendor.

### Gate 3: The employee is specifically authorized

Only the currently assigned employee or an expressly authorized replacement may record. Reassignment ends the former employee's authority.

### Gate 4: The decision-maker is identifiable

The person making a customer decision must be the customer or an authorized representative. The confidence required in that identity should rise with the sensitivity of the location, subject, and intended audience.

Possession of a message or link alone should not be treated as conclusive authority when the recording affects a home, a person, a minor, confidential information, or public publication.

### Gate 5: The decision-maker has authority over the subject

The customer must have authority over the premises, property, person, or private interest involved. Location presence does not prove authority. Payment for a service does not automatically prove authority over every person or object that may be captured.

### Gate 6: Recording is explained separately from service

The customer must understand:

- that recording is requested;
- why it is requested;
- what portions of the work will be captured;
- whether people, voices, documents, addresses, or identifying details may appear;
- whether audio is included;
- who may initially view the proof; and
- that public publication is a separate later decision.

### Gate 7: Recording permission is affirmative

Silence, inactivity, account creation, payment, presence, prior service, acceptance of general terms, or failure to object does not grant recording permission.

### Gate 8: The scope is narrow

The recording must be limited to what is reasonably needed to prove the service stage. Private rooms, unrelated possessions, documents, screens, faces, license plates, access codes, security systems, medical information, financial information, and other sensitive details must be excluded unless they are essential and separately authorized.

### Gate 9: Protected non-participants are addressed

If an identifiable bystander or other non-participant may be captured, recording must wait until that person is outside the frame, valid authority is established, or the content can be reliably de-identified.

### Gate 10: Minors receive heightened protection

An adult customer's permission is not automatically permission to record every minor who may be present. Recording a minor requires verified guardian authority and a service-related necessity. Public publication involving a minor requires a separate, exact-media guardian decision and should remain exceptional.

### Gate 11: Audio is treated separately

Audio can reveal conversations and identities beyond the visual service proof. Audio should remain off unless it is necessary for the service purpose and every person whose identifiable speech may be captured has appropriate authority or permission.

### Gate 12: Conditions still match at the moment of capture

Permission is not a one-time substitute for judgment. The employee must stop or narrow the recording if:

- the location changes;
- the subject changes;
- an unexpected person enters;
- sensitive information becomes visible;
- the purpose changes;
- the customer withdraws;
- safety is compromised; or
- the employee is uncertain whether the authorization still applies.

### Location-specific behavior

#### Vendor-controlled location

The vendor may authorize private recording of vendor-controlled work areas and vendor-owned subjects when no customer-controlled or third-party private interest is captured.

If the recording identifies a customer, customer property, customer information, or a customer-specific service, the customer must be informed and any required customer authority must be obtained regardless of where the recording occurs.

#### Customer residence

Customer recording authority is always required. Private areas receive the highest level of restraint. Public publication must never be inferred from permission to document the work.

#### Customer business

An authorized business representative must approve the recording. That representative may authorize the business premises and business property, but not automatically every employee, visitor, customer, document, screen, or confidential activity present.

#### Public or shared location

The fact that a service occurs in public does not eliminate privacy, likeness, confidentiality, or property concerns. Reliance proof must remain focused on the service and avoid identifiable non-participants.

## 7. Permissions Required Before Publication

Publication is an optional, separate decision made after the actual media exists.

Public publication requires all of the following:

1. **Valid recording authority existed** when the media was captured.
2. **The completed media is available for review** by every participant whose nondelegable permission is required.
3. **The exact media is identified.** Approval of a description, category, or future recording is not enough.
4. **The intended audience is clear.** Private proof, limited sharing, customer-visible proof, and public publication are different audiences.
5. **Customer approval exists** for customer-controlled people, property, premises, identity, or private interests.
6. **Vendor approval exists** for official representation of the vendor's work and brand.
7. **Employee approval exists** when the employee's face, voice, personal story, or other personal identity is part of the publication rather than incidental hands-on work.
8. **No unauthorized non-participant is identifiable.**
9. **The media accurately represents the service** and is not deceptive through omission, staging, editing, sequencing, or labeling.
10. **Neutral review requirements are satisfied.**
11. **No active withdrawal, dispute, safety restriction, or legal hold blocks publication.**
12. **The audience does not exceed the narrowest valid permission.**

### Audience hierarchy

From narrowest to broadest:

1. Restricted evidence access
2. Vendor and assigned employee
3. Customer and vendor
4. Limited authenticated sharing
5. Public access

A participant may authorize a narrower audience without authorizing a broader one. Reliance may narrow access for safety or integrity. Reliance may never widen access beyond participant authority.

### Private is the default

New proof remains private unless public publication is affirmatively approved after exact-media review.

Declining publication must not:

- convert an approval into a refusal of service;
- erase valid proof that work occurred;
- lower a customer's standing;
- create a negative review;
- reduce the customer's ability to receive service records; or
- pressure the customer into broader exposure.

## 8. Permissions That Can Be Withdrawn

### Future recording permission

The customer may withdraw permission for any recording that has not yet occurred. Recording must stop as soon as the withdrawal is known.

### Publication permission

The customer may withdraw or narrow public publication permission for customer-controlled content. The vendor may withdraw vendor approval for public business representation. An identifiable employee may withdraw public use of the employee's personal likeness.

Withdrawal should result in prompt restriction from the affected audience. It does not require Reliance to pretend the earlier permission never existed.

### Optional sharing permission

Permission for limited sharing may be narrowed or ended unless preservation is required for an active dispute, safety matter, fraud inquiry, or other legitimate evidence need.

### Permission that cannot be retroactively withdrawn

A participant cannot retroactively change the historical fact that:

- a decision was made;
- recording was authorized at a particular time;
- a recording occurred within the then-active scope;
- a notice was sent;
- a review or moderation decision occurred; or
- content was public during a prior period.

The continuing use of content can be withdrawn. Historical truth cannot be rewritten.

### Withdrawal does not mean immediate destruction

Withdrawal from recording or publication is different from deletion of all evidence.

Reliance should preserve only the minimum restricted evidence reasonably needed to establish:

- the prior decision;
- the withdrawal;
- the work-record history;
- the reason for any continued restriction or retention; and
- the final disposition.

Retention must not become a hidden form of continued publication.

## 9. Permissions That Become Permanent Evidence

In this architecture, "permanent evidence" means the historical record of a decision is never silently overwritten during its legitimate evidence lifecycle. It does not mean every item must be kept forever.

The following become permanent historical facts:

- who requested a decision;
- who was asked to decide;
- the authority claimed by the decision-maker;
- the identity-confidence level used for that decision;
- what service, location, subject, employee, purpose, and audience were covered;
- what information was presented before the decision;
- whether the decision was granted, declined, expired, superseded, or withdrawn;
- when the decision occurred;
- which exact media was later approved or rejected for publication;
- what audience was authorized;
- what later restriction or withdrawal occurred;
- who performed manager and admin review;
- what content was made public and for what period;
- what notifications were attempted or delivered; and
- what final disposition occurred.

A later decision adds a new historical event. It never replaces the earlier one.

## 10. Events That Always Create an Immutable Audit Record

### Authority events

- Vendor responsibility is established for a work record.
- An employee is assigned, reassigned, released, or removed.
- A customer or representative claims decision-making authority.
- Guardian or representative authority is accepted, rejected, or questioned.

### Permission events

- Recording permission is requested.
- The request is viewed.
- Recording permission is granted, declined, expired, superseded, or withdrawn.
- Publication permission is requested for completed media.
- Exact media is approved, rejected, partially approved, or limited to a narrower audience.
- Audio permission is granted or denied.
- A protected-person restriction is identified.

### Recording events

- Recording begins or ends for each service stage.
- Recording is blocked because authority, location, assignment, or scope is not valid.
- A recording is confirmed as the intended stage proof.
- A recording is replaced, removed from active use, or marked as accidental capture.
- A recording is submitted for review.

### Review and publication events

- A manager approves, rejects, or requests correction.
- An admin approves, rejects, flags, restricts, or unpublishes.
- The audience changes.
- Content first becomes public.
- Public content is withdrawn, restricted, or restored.
- A review is submitted, moderated, published, restricted, or removed.

### Integrity and dispute events

- A participant reports a privacy, identity, safety, accuracy, or authority concern.
- Access is paused during review.
- Evidence is placed under preservation.
- A dispute outcome or appeal outcome is recorded.
- A deletion request, retention decision, purge, or inability to purge is recorded.
- A material notice fails, is retried, or is confirmed delivered.

Immutable history must record the event without turning sensitive content itself into unnecessary permanent data.

## 11. Events That Always Notify Another Participant

Notifications must be timely, understandable, specific to the work record, and limited to information the recipient is entitled to receive.

| Event | Participants who must be notified |
|---|---|
| Recording permission requested | Customer; Vendor |
| Recording permission granted or declined | Customer; Vendor; Assigned Employee |
| Recording permission expires or is withdrawn | Customer; Vendor; Assigned Employee |
| Employee assigned or reassigned | Assigned Employee; Replaced Employee; Vendor |
| Recording blocked for authority or scope | Assigned Employee; Vendor |
| Employee submits completed proof | Vendor |
| Manager requests correction | Employee; Vendor |
| Manager approves for neutral review | Vendor; Assigned Employee |
| Admin approves, rejects, flags, or restricts | Vendor; Customer when customer access or publication is affected |
| Exact-media publication approval requested | Customer; Vendor; Identifiable Employee when applicable |
| Publication approved and content becomes public | Customer; Vendor; Identifiable Employee when applicable |
| Publication permission withdrawn | Customer; Vendor; Admin when action is required |
| Content is unpublished or audience is narrowed | Customer; Vendor; Identifiable Employee when applicable |
| Privacy, safety, identity, or authority dispute opened | Affected Customer; Vendor; Admin |
| Dispute or appeal resolved | Every participant directly affected by the outcome |
| Deletion or purge request completed or denied | Requesting participant; Vendor when the work record is affected |
| A required notice cannot be delivered | Vendor or Admin responsible for follow-up; affected participant through an available alternate path |

A notification is evidence that Reliance attempted to inform someone. It is not a substitute for affirmative permission.

## 12. Consent Lifecycle

Recording consent and publication consent have separate lifecycles.

### Recording consent states

1. **Not requested**
2. **Pending**
3. **Granted for a defined scope**
4. **Declined**
5. **Expired**
6. **Superseded by a new request**
7. **Withdrawn for future capture**
8. **Completed**
9. **Disputed**
10. **Restricted for evidence only**

### Publication consent states

1. **Not requested**
2. **Private by default**
3. **Exact-media review pending**
4. **Approved for a defined audience**
5. **Partially approved**
6. **Declined**
7. **Withdrawn or narrowed**
8. **Restricted by admin**
9. **Disputed**
10. **No longer retained for distribution**

### Lifecycle rules

- A recording decision must not automatically move publication forward.
- A publication decision must reference completed media.
- Expiration never becomes approval.
- Replacing media ends any publication approval tied to the prior media.
- Reassignment does not silently transfer personal employee permissions.
- A scope change requires a new decision.
- A broader audience requires a new decision.
- A narrower audience may be applied immediately for safety.
- Withdrawal creates a new state and preserves the earlier history.
- Disputed content moves to the narrowest reasonable access state until resolved.

## 13. Principles for Future Consent Features

Future consent features should follow these principles:

### Purpose limitation

Collect and use proof only for the stated service-related purpose.

### Separate decisions

Service, recording, audio, publication, reviews, promotional use, and retention are distinct decisions.

### Decision at the right time

Ask for recording authority before capture and publication authority after the exact media exists.

### Least-public default

Begin with the narrowest practical audience. Broader visibility must be earned through clear authority.

### Identity confidence proportional to risk

The more sensitive the place, subject, or audience, the stronger the confidence required that the right person is deciding.

### Authority over convenience

A fast decision from the wrong person is invalid. Convenience must not replace authority.

### Evidence without coercion

Reliance should protect vendors with credible proof without pressuring customers into public exposure.

### Reversible distribution

Public or shared access should be capable of being narrowed without destroying legitimate historical evidence.

### Immutable history, minimal content

Preserve the decision trail while minimizing the sensitive material held within that trail.

### Exact-media approval

Permission for a concept, service category, or future recording is not permission for the completed media.

### Role independence

Vendor, employee, customer, admin, and Reliance decisions remain distinct even when one person holds more than one account role.

### Protected-person awareness

The architecture must anticipate bystanders, minors, household members, customer employees, visitors, and others who did not initiate the service.

### Comprehension

A valid decision requires understandable purpose, scope, audience, and consequences. Complexity should be carried by Reliance, not pushed onto the customer.

### Neutrality

Reliance protects the process and the evidence. It does not advocate for a participant by manipulating consent, ratings, visibility, or history.

### Universal vocabulary

Future designs should describe subject, location, authority, purpose, audience, and lifecycle rather than creating industry-specific consent systems.

## 14. Principles That Must Never Be Violated

Reliance must never:

1. Treat silence, inactivity, or a missed message as approval.
2. Treat account registration or general platform use as recording permission.
3. Treat recording permission as publication permission.
4. Publish customer-controlled content before the customer can review the exact media.
5. Allow a vendor, employee, admin, or Reliance to impersonate the customer's decision.
6. Allow a customer to authorize a non-participant's rights without valid authority.
7. Allow an admin decision to manufacture missing participant permission.
8. Expand an audience beyond the narrowest valid authorization.
9. Continue recording after consent is withdrawn or conditions materially change.
10. Pressure a customer to accept public publication as a condition of ordinary service.
11. Penalize a customer for choosing private proof.
12. Rewrite, backdate, or silently replace a consent decision.
13. Destroy relevant evidence merely because a participant disputes it.
14. Preserve public access merely because historical evidence must be retained.
15. Use proof-of-service media for unrelated promotion, surveillance, profiling, or training without separate authority.
16. Allow edits, captions, sequencing, or labels to create a misleading representation of the work.
17. Treat location presence as proof of consent or legal authority.
18. Treat manager or admin approval as customer publication consent.
19. Treat a prior work order as permission for a future one.
20. Resolve uncertainty by choosing the broader audience.

## 15. Disagreement and Conflict Rules

Disagreements are resolved by role authority, not by majority vote.

| Disagreement | Governing outcome |
|---|---|
| Customer and vendor disagree about whether recording may begin | Recording does not begin. The service may proceed without Reliance recording if the parties choose. |
| Customer permits private proof but vendor wants public proof | Proof remains private. |
| Customer previously approved publication and later withdraws | Public access is restricted promptly; the decision history remains preserved. |
| Vendor wants proof retained but customer requests destruction | Public access stops if withdrawn. Minimum evidence may remain restricted while the retention question is reviewed. |
| Employee believes recording exceeds scope | Recording pauses. The vendor may clarify, narrow the scope, reassign, or proceed without recording. |
| Vendor says a stage is accurate and customer disputes it | Original proof and the dispute are preserved. Public display may be paused when the dispute concerns privacy, identity, or material misrepresentation. |
| Manager rejects employee proof | The employee may correct the affected stage; the original decision history remains. |
| Admin rejects content that participants approved | Content does not become public through Reliance. Participant permission is necessary but does not guarantee platform eligibility. |
| Customer demands publication that vendor declines | Reliance does not publish the content as the vendor's official proof. |
| Participants dispute who had authority | Recording or publication pauses until authority is established. |
| A minor or bystander concern arises | Privacy protection takes priority; access narrows while the concern is reviewed. |
| A participant challenges an admin decision | A neutral appeal may review consistency, evidence, and role authority without rewriting the original decision. |

### Conflict-handling sequence

1. Stop any new recording or audience expansion.
2. Move affected content to the narrowest reasonable access state.
3. Preserve the existing evidence and decision history.
4. Notify directly affected participants.
5. Identify which participant holds the disputed authority.
6. Separate privacy and consent questions from service-quality disagreements.
7. Decide platform eligibility without pretending to decide external legal ownership.
8. Record the outcome and any continuing restriction.
9. Permit a neutral appeal when the result materially affects a participant.

## 16. Balancing Privacy, Protection, and Neutrality

Reliance should balance interests through order of operations, not through vague compromise.

### First: Safety and valid authority

No proof objective overrides personal safety, valid authority, protection of minors, or the need to stop unauthorized capture.

### Second: Customer privacy and choice

The customer controls customer-specific recording and publication decisions. Private proof must remain a complete and respected choice.

### Third: Vendor protection

The vendor is entitled to credible evidence that work was assigned, performed, reviewed, disputed, corrected, and completed. Customer withdrawal from public display must not erase truthful service history.

### Fourth: Employee protection

The employee is entitled to clear scope, valid assignment, protection from instructions that exceed consent, preservation of accurate work evidence, and control over personal likeness beyond ordinary service activity.

### Fifth: Platform neutrality

Reliance protects the rules, not a preferred outcome. It must:

- preserve truthful evidence;
- enforce the narrowest active permission;
- keep ratings and publication separate from consent pressure;
- distinguish a privacy dispute from a service-quality dispute;
- apply the same standards across industries and account types; and
- avoid turning moderation into ownership of participant decisions.

### The balancing rule

When interests conflict:

- privacy controls distribution;
- valid evidence preserves history;
- participant authority controls personal decisions;
- neutral review controls Reliance eligibility; and
- uncertainty produces restriction, not expansion.

## 17. Universal Application Across Industries

The architecture changes behavior according to what is recorded, not according to the vendor category.

### Person-centered services

Examples: barber, tutor, photographer.

The service may inherently involve an identifiable person. Recording and public publication require close attention to likeness, voice, minors, personal surroundings, and exact-media approval.

### Property and asset services

Examples: mechanic, appliance repair, locksmith, pet groomer.

The proof may focus on an object or animal, but identifiers, ownership details, access methods, keys, addresses, documents, and people must remain outside the recording unless necessary and authorized.

### Premises-based services

Examples: electrician, plumber, house cleaner, landscaper, pool company, HVAC.

The service may reveal the layout, security, condition, possessions, occupants, or location of a home or business. The recording must remain tightly focused on the serviced area and must not convert private premises into public content without exact-media approval.

### Future industries

A new industry does not require a new philosophy. Reliance asks:

- Who or what is the subject?
- Who controls it?
- What proof is necessary?
- Who may be affected?
- Who may see it?
- What may later be withdrawn or retained?

If those questions are answered, the same architecture applies.

## 18. Universal Decision Test

Before any future recording or publication feature is accepted, it should pass this test:

1. Is the purpose directly connected to proof of service?
2. Is the subject defined narrowly?
3. Is the correct decision-maker identified?
4. Does that person have authority over every affected right?
5. Are recording and publication separate?
6. Can the customer choose private proof without penalty?
7. Can every required participant review the exact media before public use?
8. Are minors, bystanders, audio, private spaces, documents, and identifiers addressed?
9. Can future use be withdrawn or narrowed?
10. Will the historical decision remain trustworthy after withdrawal?
11. Will every consequential event create evidence and appropriate notice?
12. Can Reliance restrict access without inventing participant permission?
13. Does the same rule work for a home, business, person, vehicle, animal, and future service?
14. Does uncertainty lead to a safer and narrower outcome?

If any answer is no, the feature does not conform to this architecture.

---

## 19. Final Architecture Statement

Reliance should be trusted because it proves service without claiming more authority than it has.

The customer controls customer-specific recording and publication choices. The vendor controls the legitimate service purpose, its workforce, and its official business representation. The employee controls the moment of capture and may stop when conditions are unsafe or outside scope. The admin controls platform eligibility but cannot create consent. Reliance preserves the integrity of the process, enforces the narrowest valid permission, and keeps private proof distinct from public promotion.

The timeless rule is:

> Record only with proper authority. Publish only with exact-media permission. Preserve the truth of every decision. When uncertain, protect privacy and narrow access.

Future Reliance decisions should follow this architecture unless a later formally adopted consent architecture supersedes it.
