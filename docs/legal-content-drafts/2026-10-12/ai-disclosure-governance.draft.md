# Reliance AI-Assisted Features Disclosure and Governance

## Customer-facing disclosure

Reliance may use AI-assisted tools to help summarize information, organize records, flag content for attention, suggest workflow guidance, or draft material for a person to review. AI output may be incomplete, inaccurate, or inappropriate for the circumstances.

A qualified person remains responsible for consequential decisions. AI does not make the final decision about:

- Reliance Audit or Public-display eligibility;
- Customer or Employee consent, assent, or identity;
- recording or Public visibility authorization;
- content reports, disputes, or privacy requests;
- employment, assignment, or retaliation matters;
- workmanship, job-site safety, licensure, insurance, or code compliance; or
- legal rights or professional advice.

Do not rely on AI output as legal, safety, trade, or other professional advice. Use the available correction, report, or human-support path when output appears wrong.

## Internal governance requirements

Before an AI use is enabled, Reliance should document its purpose, owner, permitted inputs, provider, model or service class, human reviewer, output guard, retention, access, testing, monitoring, and rollback path. The implementation should minimize personal information, exclude secrets and unnecessary precise location, and preserve an audit trail appropriate to the risk.

Reliance should test for factual errors, unsafe instructions, discrimination, privacy leakage, prompt injection, unsupported legal conclusions, and automation bias. High-impact or rights-affecting output must not bypass established server-side authorization or human review.

## Provider statements

Reliance should maintain a verified provider inventory and review provider terms, data controls, security, retention, subprocessors, and model-improvement settings. Public wording must not claim that a provider never trains on, retains, or uses data unless Reliance has verified that exact commitment for the configured service and can continue to support it.

## New-purpose rule

Information collected for service delivery, consent evidence, Private Proof, location verification, reports, or support must not be secretly repurposed for a materially new AI use. Reliance should provide appropriate notice and obtain affirmative consent when required before a materially different use.

## User-submitted content

Users should avoid placing passwords, one-time codes, bearer tokens, full signed URLs, payment credentials, or unnecessary sensitive personal information into an AI input. AI output should not expose another person's private information or internal security detail.

## Activation dependencies

- Current provider and data-flow inventory.
- Verified contractual and configuration controls.
- Risk classification, evals, output guards, human-review ownership, and incident response.
- Feature-specific just-in-time disclosure where needed.
- Privacy, retention, access, and deletion behavior tested end to end.
- Product Owner and attorney review for high-impact or sensitive uses.
